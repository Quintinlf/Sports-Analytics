Lesson 1

What you're trying to do

Win chips. You win the chips in the middle (the pot) in one of two ways:

  • At the end, the cards are turned over (the showdown) and your best five cards beat theirs, or
  • You bet and they give up (fold) before the end. Then nobody shows anything and the pot is yours.

That second way is why poker isn't just luck: you can win with bad cards, and lose with good ones.

Lesson 2

Your cards and the table's cards

You get two cards only you see. Then five cards are dealt face up in the middle, shared by both players:

  • three at once: the flop
  • then one more: the turn
  • then the last one: the river

Your hand is the best five cards you can make from your two plus the five on the table. You can use both of yours, one, or none.

Lesson 3

Which hands beat which

Best at the top. Suits never beat each other. If two hands are the same kind, the higher cards win; if they're exactly equal, the pot is split.

Practice: which hand wins?

Lesson 4

How one hand goes (two players)

  1. Blinds. Before any cards, both players put in a small forced bet so there's something to play for: the small blind (1 chip here) and the big blind (2 chips).
  2. The button. With two players, the player with the dealer button pays the small blind. The button switches every hand.
  3. Before the flop the button acts first. After the flop the other player acts first on every round, so the button gets to see what they do before choosing.
  4. Four rounds of betting: before the flop, after the flop, after the turn, after the river.
  5. Showdown. If nobody folded, both show and the best five cards win the pot.

Lesson 5

Your choices on your turn

Check
Pass without betting. Only possible when nobody has bet this round.
Bet
Put chips in when nobody has yet. The other player must call, raise or fold.
Call
Match the bet in front of you.
Raise
Bet more than the bet in front of you. In no-limit, a raise must be at least as big as the last bet or raise, and you can go up to everything you have (all-in).
Fold
Give up the hand. The chips you already put in stay in the pot.

If you can't afford the whole bet, you can still call all-in; you can only win as much from them as you put in.

Lesson 6

Which hands to play

  • With only two players you play many more hands than at a full table: the blinds come around every hand, so waiting only for great cards bleeds chips.
  • Good hands to start with: any pair, two high cards (ace, king, queen, jack, ten), and an ace with a card of the same suit.
  • Hands like 7-2 or 9-3 of different suits are the ones to fold, unless you can see the flop for free.
  • Being last to act after the flop (having the button) helps: you see their choice first.

Lesson 7

The price of a call

Say the pot is 10 and they bet 5. Calling costs you 5, and if you win you get the whole pot: 10 + their 5 + your 5 = 20.

You pay 5 to win 20, so you need to win at least 5 ÷ 20 = 25% of the time.

If your hand wins more often than that, calling makes chips over many hands, even when you lose this one. If it wins less, fold. The coach at the table works this out every time it stops you, and tells you how often your hand wins against the cards that opponent plays.

Lesson 8

Every bet needs a reason

  • A value bet: you bet because a worse hand will call. You get paid.
  • A bluff: you bet because a better hand will fold. You steal the pot.

If neither is true, checking is usually better. Which one works depends on who you're playing: a player who never folds is great to value bet and terrible to bluff. That's the last lesson.
